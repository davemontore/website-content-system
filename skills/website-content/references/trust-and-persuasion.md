# Trust, persuasion, and conversion

Use these modes only when they are part of the page's declared job.

## Establish trust

Trust content gives visitors enough evidence to make their own confidence decision.

- Answer who is responsible, what they do, who it is for, what it costs or requires, and what happens after contact or commitment.
- State policies, limitations, safeguards, credentials, and experience specifically enough to verify.
- Prefer a relevant case, process, named qualification, source, or clear policy over badges and adjectives.
- Explain privacy and security through actual protections, access, retention, and user control when known.
- Make contact details, ownership, dates, and correction paths easy to find where relevant.
- Do not hide disqualifying information until after the visitor acts.

Trust is not a warm-up for pressure. A visitor may reasonably decide not to proceed.

## Persuade

Persuasion should connect a real audience concern to evidence and reasoning.

1. Name the current belief, problem, or tradeoff accurately.
2. Present the claim the page wants the visitor to consider.
3. Support it with concrete evidence, demonstration, comparison, or reasoning.
4. Address the strongest reasonable objection.
5. Offer a proportionate next step.

Represent alternatives fairly. Do not create a weak opponent merely to make the preferred option look strong.

## Convert

Conversion content helps a qualified visitor decide and act without obscuring consequences.

- State the offer and intended audience early.
- Translate features into consequences only when the connection is true and relevant.
- Use verified proof near the claim it supports.
- Make pricing, commitment, eligibility, cancellation, and the next step clear.
- Write calls to action that describe the action or result.
- Match urgency to a real deadline or constraint. Never manufacture scarcity.
- Address objections with facts rather than reassurance.

Choose a page flow from the questions the visitor needs answered. A problem-solution or before-after structure may help, but no named copywriting framework is mandatory.

## Specificity checks

- Could a direct competitor use the same positioning sentence unchanged? If yes, add a real differentiator or make a narrower claim.
- Does each major benefit connect to a feature, policy, process, or result?
- Does every number say what was counted, for whom, and when when those details matter?
- Is the requested action clear before the visitor clicks?

Do not require a number in every section, force second-person language into every sentence, or choose a fixed sales-page sequence. Those are situational tactics, not quality laws.
