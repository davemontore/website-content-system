# Universal standards

Apply these standards to every surface and page job.

## Priority order

1. Tell the truth and represent evidence accurately.
2. Help the visitor understand or complete the task.
3. Include information that is useful for the page's job.
4. Use personality where the context can safely carry it.
5. Improve discoverability without weakening the first four priorities.

## Evidence

- Distinguish observed facts, interpretations, opinions, and recommendations.
- Name a source when a factual claim depends on one.
- Explain material limits, dates, samples, exclusions, or uncertainty close to the affected claim.
- Treat missing evidence as missing. Do not turn it into zero, certainty, consensus, or a superlative.
- Use numbers only when they are verified and meaningful. A page does not become specific by adding decorative statistics.
- Do not publish testimonials, review language, credentials, guarantees, or comparisons unless their accuracy and use are authorized.

## Information hierarchy

- Start with the answer, action, or definition the visitor came for.
- Order details by dependency and importance, not by the organization's internal structure.
- Make headings carry information. A visitor scanning only headings should understand the route through the page.
- Keep setup and eligibility requirements before the action that depends on them.
- Put qualifications next to the claim they limit.

## Language

- Prefer familiar, precise words. Define necessary terms on first use.
- Give sentences a clear actor and action when that improves understanding.
- Keep one main idea per sentence. Vary length naturally rather than imposing a mechanical target.
- Use active voice when the actor matters. Use passive voice when the outcome matters more or the actor is unknown.
- Repeat the correct term instead of cycling through synonyms.
- Use concrete nouns and verbs before adjectives and slogans.
- Preserve a real voice, including measured uncertainty or humor, when it does not obstruct the page's job.

## Remove empty writing

Cut text that only announces importance, describes its own structure, claims a tone, or repeats a point without adding meaning. Common symptoms include:

- scene-setting that delays the answer
- generic aspiration without a fact or action
- headings such as "Overview" or "Why it matters" when a specific heading is available
- vague trust badges or unsupported quality claims
- fake urgency
- padded transitions and ceremonial conclusions
- labels from internal systems that do not help the visitor decide or act

Do not maintain a rigid banned-word list. A word is a problem when it is vague, false, overused, mismatched to the audience, or replacing a more exact statement.

## Voice and accessibility

- Match tone to the visitor's situation. Failure, risk, money, privacy, health, and destructive actions usually need calm directness.
- Do not rely on humor, idiom, color, position, or visual context to carry necessary meaning.
- Use descriptive link and control text that remains understandable out of context.
- Keep terminology and interaction patterns consistent across the experience.
- Respect the user's locale and established spelling. Ask only when the distinction matters.
