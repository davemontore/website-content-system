# Page jobs

Classify the page by the change it should create for the visitor. Topic and format do not determine the job by themselves.

| Job | The visitor should leave able to... | Typical visitor question | Evidence of success |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inform | know an accurate fact, explanation, or state of affairs | What is true here? | Correct understanding |
| Orient | know where they are and where relevant material lives | Where am I, and where do I go? | Confident navigation |
| Teach | understand a concept or carry out a process | How does this work? | Comprehension or successful practice |
| Enable | complete an action in an interface | What do I do now? | Task completion and recovery |
| Establish trust | judge whether a person, organization, or system is credible for this situation | Can I rely on this, and on what basis? | An informed confidence decision |
| Explore | discover related material without being pushed toward one outcome | What else is here? | Meaningful discovery |
| Persuade | reconsider a belief, preference, or interpretation | Why should I see this differently? | A changed view supported by reasons |
| Convert | take a specific, voluntary action | Is this worth doing now, and what happens next? | A well-informed action |

## Hybrid pages

Use a primary and secondary job, not a vague list of equal goals.

Examples:

- A museum collection page may primarily help visitors explore and secondarily orient them to themes.
- A public-service eligibility page may primarily inform and secondarily enable an application.
- A consultancy service page may primarily establish trust and secondarily convert qualified visitors.

When goals compete, the primary job wins. A conversion request must not obscure eligibility limits. Search wording must not distort historical facts. Playful brand voice must not weaken an error message.

## Page job versus content type

The same page can contain several content types. A conversion page may still need a factual comparison table and plain instructions. An informational article may include navigation and a low-pressure invitation to explore related material.

Classify each section again when local purpose differs from the page's primary job.
